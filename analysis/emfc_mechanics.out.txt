   span sensitivity 127 ppm per mrad of theta0 -> d(theta0)/dT <= 3.9 urad/K for 0.5 ppm/K
   a 0.1 K top-bottom gradient change bends the Al lever ~7 urad (mean slope) -> 0.9 ppm span shift:
   vertical gradients in the lever must stay stable to a few tens of mK (thermal symmetry, coil heat path, enclosure).
   Set theta0 ~ 0 by clamping the blades with the lever pinned at null in an assembly jig (~0.3 mrad), then trim
   electronically: the servo setpoint (optical null offset) is an adjuster with no mechanical work; theta0 = 0 is where
   the nonlinearity changes sign between two setpoints (Phase 7 characterization).

6. Lever bending, lever thermal gradient, clamp slip
   lever tip deflection at full coil force 0.67 um -> 3.0 mg, ~linear in load (span, calibrated);
   the 11 % K(P) change over the range leaves ~0.08 mg nonlinearity. Vane at the coil, lever deep.
   coil heat Km 0.9: 143 mW x 3.8 K/W x 0.5 (half flows along the lever, ASSUMED) -> 269 mK arm-to-arm -> ratio 6.35 ppm at full load, ~0.83 mg nonlinearity (P ~ W^2)
   coil heat Km 1.6: 45 mW x 3.8 K/W x 0.5 (half flows along the lever, ASSUMED) -> 85 mK arm-to-arm -> ratio 2.01 ppm at full load, ~0.26 mg nonlinearity (P ~ W^2)
   -> A constant-power coil drive turns this into a calibrated constant. Ti/SS levers would be 8-9x worse (alpha/k).
   clamp slip along x at the fulcrum changes the ratio by slip/a: 1 ppm = 10 nm -> clamps must not slip:
   preload >= 100x (shear / mu), bonded + bolted blade clamps, no shear path through dowel clearance.
   Steel blades in Al clamps (documented material mix): 12.6 ppm/K mismatch is taken as blade strain (mN-level), no slip.
