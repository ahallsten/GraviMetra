6. Power and thermal
   FS (linear drive, full load): P 6.1 mW -> coil rise 58 mK over the pot (gap 79.4 + convection 6.1 + mount 20 mW/K); pot/magnet rise ~49 mK at R_pot 8.0 K/W
   constant power at F_max: P 8.8 mW -> coil rise 84 mK over the pot (gap 79.4 + convection 6.1 + mount 20 mW/K); pot/magnet rise ~71 mK at R_pot 8.0 K/W
   Linear drive: magnet temperature follows load. 0 -> 520 g changes coil power by 6 mW; ~75 % reaches the pot -> magnet +37 mK -> span error at 520 g 6.7 mg (Recoma 28) / 0.19 mg (TC16), with a thermal time constant of minutes (creep-like).
   -> constant coil power is required at full range (it is optional for dosing loads of a few grams).
   Constant-power options (implementation chosen in the servo/measurand study):
     a) +/-I0 bipolar PWM in one winding: P = I0^2 R constant = 9 mW; force ~ (2D-1); Km unchanged; measurand is duty x I0.
     b) split winding, 80 % force + 20 % non-inductive bifilar heater: Km x0.894 = 3.96; heater tops total power up to 11 mW.
     b) split winding, 70 % force + 30 % non-inductive bifilar heater: Km x0.837 = 3.71; heater tops total power up to 13 mW.
     c) separate heater resistor on the bobbin: Km unchanged, but heat enters at another spot (residual
        load-dependent gradient and a transient after each load step).

   solid bobbin: c = 2 pi r sigma t int(B_r^2 dz) = 2.03 N s/m at the coil -> 45.7 mN m s/rad at the fulcrum; critical (open loop, empty) 17.8 -> zeta 2.6
   a single axial slit in the bobbin removes it (zeta ~0). Choice goes to the servo study; both use the same part.
   With a current-source drive the winding itself adds no damping (open-circuit for back-EMF).

12. Alternatives to a plain permanent magnet (user questions 2026-11-22)
   The Ø31.75 x 12.70 mm SmCo disc supplies H_cB x l_m = 10160 A-turns of MMF at zero power.
   12a. Electromagnet instead of the magnet (soft-iron core in its place, field winding in the cavity,
        linear steel = lower bound on power, same moving coil and Kf):
        same pot envelope                  window    70 mm^2: needs   1792 A-turns ->  46.5 A/mm^2,   179.5 W (vs 6 mW coil), surface rise ~1969 K at 10 W/m^2K
        enlarged pot, OD 100 x 40 mm core  window   863 mm^2: needs   1857 A-turns ->   3.9 A/mm^2,    22.0 W (vs 6 mW coil), surface rise ~69 K at 10 W/m^2K
        -> 10^3-10^4 x the heat the whole cell budget is built around, inside the cell. Plus: field depends on
        a second current (1 ppm regulation = the same difficulty as the measurand itself), iron hysteresis
        (power-up remanence state) and iron permeability vs temperature. Rejected: a bigger accuracy problem.
   12b. Magnet + trim winding to hold Kf over the 15 K swing (0.50 %): 43 A-turns, up to 104 mW varying with temperature. It still needs the magnet temperature (or a ppm field sensor,
        which does not exist at this price), so it only moves the firmware correction into hardware. No gain.
   12c. Two coils on the lever:
        - opposed pair for constant power: each has half the copper (Km/sqrt2), so constant power is 18 mW vs 9 mW for +/-I0 PWM in one winding (the 80/20 split heater is in between).
        - drift: both coils sit in the same field, so any force or ratio still carries B(T). A second coil
          cannot cancel the magnet's temperature coefficient.
   12d. In-situ alpha from two check-mass adjustments 2 K apart: u(alpha) = sqrt2 x 1.7 ppm / 2 K = 1.20 ppm/K = 0.36 % of alpha (needed: 0.6 %).
   12d. In-situ alpha from two check-mass adjustments 5 K apart: u(alpha) = sqrt2 x 1.7 ppm / 5 K = 0.48 ppm/K = 0.14 % of alpha (needed: 0.6 %).
        -> the coefficient of the finished cell (magnet + coil + steel + electronics) is measurable to better
        than any vendor value; the hard part is the magnet's temperature at each instant (lag, gradients).
   12e. Passive shunt of temperature-compensator alloy (Carpenter Temperature Compensator 30, 30 % Ni-Fe,
        permeability falls with temperature, -51..71 C; stock strip/bar). It diverts flux fraction s from the gap
        and diverts less as it warms. First order: s x k = |alpha_net|, k = relative change of shunt flux per K:
        k 0.5 %/K -> shunt carries 6.7 % of gap flux -> Km loss ~6.7 %
        k 1.0 %/K -> shunt carries 3.4 % of gap flux -> Km loss ~3.4 %
        k 2.0 %/K -> shunt carries 1.7 % of gap flux -> Km loss ~1.7 %
        k is not published (Carpenter's pages give no curves): measure on the rig. Trimmed by shunt cross-section.
        Residual after trimming is set by the curvature of mu(T) over 15-30 C [ASSUMED +/-10-30 ppm/K until measured].
